From forecast to parents' phones by 6:45 AM.

  1. 5:30

    The sky is read

    EventBridge Scheduler wakes a Step Functions workflow that fetches the hourly PM2.5 forecast for the school's exact location.

  2. 5:31

    The day is re-planned

    Each outdoor PE period searches for the cleanest slot, respecting playground capacity and teacher clashes. Younger classes pick first.

  3. 5:31

    Every move is checked

    Cedar policies, the AWS open-source policy language, decide each outdoor slot: GRAP stages, NAQI bands, classes 1–5, asthma-watch kids. The AI never decides who goes outside.

  4. 5:32

    The words are written

    A Strands agent on Amazon Bedrock turns the plan into a calm Hindi and English notice and a principal brief, citing the rule behind each change.

  5. 6:15

    The principal approves

    One tap. If nobody responds by the deadline, only the changes GRAP requires are applied, and nothing advisory goes out unapproved.

  6. 6:45

    Parents know before the bus

    A WhatsApp-ready message for the class group, a Hindi voice note from Amazon Polly, and a parent page with their child's day.

    Honest answers.

    Is a forecast accurate enough to plan a school day?

    We checked it against CPCB ground stations. At Rohini the CAMS model read 1.2–1.6× too low in early October 2025 and 2.5–4× too low from late October through November, which would under-protect children. So CleanHour bias-corrects the forecast hour by hour to the school's nearest station. The daily shape holds in the measured data: at Rohini and R K Puram, across two seasons, moving PE to the cleanest school hour cut PM2.5 by 46–57% on the median day.

    What happens when GRAP Stage III or IV is declared?

    The stage is an input to the Cedar rules. Stage III suspends outdoor sports and Stage IV keeps every outdoor activity in. These changes are marked regulatory and apply even if the principal doesn't respond.

    Isn't 1 PM too hot for PE?

    Sometimes, and CleanHour checks. The forecast includes feels-like temperature, and Cedar heat rules (35 °C for classes 1–5, 37 °C for sports, 41 °C for everyone) forbid hot slots. When heat is the problem, PE moves to the coolest allowed hour instead. Replayed on 8 April 2025, Delhi's first heatwave, it pulled 8A's PE from 11:45 to 9:25 AM: feels-like 38 → 33 °C. Same engine, all year.

    Does the AI decide whether children go outside?

    No. Plain code picks the slots and Cedar policies decide what's allowed. The agent only writes the notices and answers questions, using read-only tools.

    Do schools need to buy air sensors, or type in timetables?

    Neither. A school needs its location and a photo of its timetable. A Strands agent on Amazon Bedrock reads the photo into periods, classes and teachers, and the school is planned at 5:30 the next morning. That's what lets it reach government schools, not just the few that can afford monitors.

    Which languages does it speak?

    Hindi and English today, including a Hindi voice note. Because the agent writes from structured facts, adding Punjabi, Urdu or Bengali is a prompt change, not a rewrite.
